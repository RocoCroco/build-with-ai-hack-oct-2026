# Demo video: shot list and script

Target length: about 2 minutes (check the Devpost form for a limit). Record on the live site,
https://workoutdraw.netlify.app, with sound on and the Netlify badge turned off.

The **Say** lines are yours to write. Each one has a question to answer in your own words.

## Before recording

- [ ] "Powered by Netlify" badge turned off (Netlify → Project configuration → General)
- [ ] Phone on Do Not Disturb, notifications hidden
- [ ] One practice workout done (so the Start screen shows a stickman with some muscle, Balance has a "+N", and History has an entry)
- [ ] The real take starts on the Start screen

## Shots

### 0 · Intro animation (0:00–0:11)

The animated intro (`workout-draw-video/videos/intro`): the stickman walks into the gym, gets stuck,
gets the idea, pulls out his phone, and the camera zooms into the app's Start screen.

- On screen (speech bubbles): "I don't know what to train today?" → "Wait… I have an idea!"
- Cut straight from the intro's last frame (the Start screen) into your recording, also on the Start screen.
Say: You know those days when you get to the gym and have absolutely no idea what to train? Or you just don't feel like planning a workout? Well, that's why I created Workout Draw.

### 1 · Start screen (0:11–0:20)

- **Show:** the Start screen. Tap the stickman: he dances. Tap again: he hits a pose.
- **Say:** _What is Workout Draw, in one sentence?_

  >Workout Draw turns choosing your workout into a game. And right here on the home screen, you can see your progress come to life.

### 2 · Setup (0:20–0:40)

- **Show:** Start → turn the time dial → pick 2–3 muscles on the body map → flip the GYM switch → open the warm-up case.
- **Say:** _What does the user choose, and what does the app decide for them?_

  > Getting started is simple. Choose how much time you have, which muscle groups you want to train, and whether you're at home or at the gym. The app figures out how many exercises you'll do. Then we draw our warm-up, and if we're lucky, we might even get to skip it!

### 3 · The draw: the heart of the app (0:40–1:05)

- **Show:** pull the lever and let the reels land. Open 2–3 cases, and let at least one roll all the way to the end with the sound.
- **Say:** _Why a slot machine and case opening? What makes it fun?_

  > Now comes my favorite part: the draw. Instead of just pressing a button and getting a random workout, you actually get to open cases and discover your exercises, one by one. It's unpredictable, it's exciting, and it makes planning a workout feel more like playing a game.

### 4 · The plan (1:05–1:20)

- **Show:** the plan with sets × reps. Tap one exercise to show its animated figure and form cues. Tap "Save photo".
- **Say:** _What do you get at the end of the draw?_

  > Once you've opened all your cases, your workout is ready! You can review every exercise, see how many sets and reps to do, check the animations, and even save the whole plan as a photo.

### 5 · Train (1:20–1:40)

- **Show:** Start workout → tick a few sets → skip ahead → Done → the stickman poses for each muscle group you trained.
- **Say:** _What happens while you train, and when you finish?_

  > From here, you just train. Follow each exercise, mark your sets as you finish them, and move through your workout. Once you're done, you'll get a summary of everything you've trained.

### 6 · Payoff (1:40–1:55)

- **Show:** back on Start: the stickman has grown. Swipe to Balance (the "+N" badges). Menu → History.
- **Say:** _How does the app keep you coming back?_

  > And here's another fun part: your progress stays with you. As you complete workouts, your stickman actually grows stronger, with bigger muscles reflecting the groups you've trained. You can also track your balance and look back at your previous workouts.

### 7 · Close (1:55–2:05)

- **Show:** your face, or the logo (`docs/logo-on-dark.png`) with the link workoutdraw.netlify.app.
- **Say:** _How did you build it (Build With AI, Claude Code), and what would you add next?_

  > I built Workout Draw with AI using Claude Code. Along the way, I learned how to plan better, research and create my own skills, and use flipped interaction to improve my workflow. I hope you like it!

## Recording tips

- Record the screen in short clips, one per shot, not one long take.
- Record your voice afterwards over the clips; it's easier than talking while tapping.
- Edit in CapCut (or similar): intro → clips → close. Cut the waiting, keep the case rolls.
- Upload to YouTube as Unlisted (or as the form asks), and open the link in a private window to check it plays.
